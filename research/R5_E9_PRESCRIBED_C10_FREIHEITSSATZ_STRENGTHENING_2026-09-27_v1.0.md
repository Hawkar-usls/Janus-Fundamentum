## Lemma

Let

\[
G=\langle e_1,\ldots,e_{10}\mid e_1e_2\cdots e_{10}=1\rangle.
\]

There is no nontrivial reduced word of length `<10` in the alphabet `e_i^{+-1}` that is the identity in `G`. The defining relator itself has length exactly 10. Hence the systole of this generating set is exactly 10.

## Proof

The relator

\[
R=e_1e_2\cdots e_{10}
\]

is cyclically reduced and contains every generator.

Assume a nontrivial reduced word `w` of length `<10` represents the identity in `G`. A word of length at most 9 cannot contain all ten distinct generators, so there exists some `e_j` not occurring in `w`.

By Magnus' Freiheitssatz, the subgroup generated by the proper subset

\[
\{e_1,\ldots,e_{10}\}\setminus\{e_j\}
\]

is a free group freely generated by that subset. Since `w` lies in this subgroup and is a nontrivial reduced free word, it cannot represent the identity. Contradiction.

Thus no nontrivial reduced identity word has length `<10`. The defining relator gives an identity word of length 10, establishing exact systole 10.

## Dependent-voltage form

Solving the relator for the designated final cycle edge gives

\[
e_{10}=(e_1e_2\cdots e_9)^{-1}.
\]

Therefore forcing the net voltage of the prescribed base C10 to identity cannot make any shorter reduced base word universally trivial. For a base closed nonbacktracking word of length `<10`, substituting the dependent `e10` expands each such letter by at most 9, so the resulting nontrivial free word has length at most `9*9=81`. This feeds directly into the fixed finite-2-group separation and conditional-expectation construction in the parent theorem candidate.

## Source binding

The theorem used here is classical, not JANUS novelty. Checked formulations include:

- Magnus' Freiheitssatz: in a one-relator group, a subset of generators omitting a generator appearing in the cyclically reduced relator freely generates its subgroup.
- Collins, *Intersections of Magnus subgroups*, formulation of the Freiheitssatz for Magnus subsets.
- Modern one-relator-group notes stating the same free-subgroup theorem.
