# JANUS external scientific review and outreach protocol

**Version:** 2026-09-23 v1.0  
**Purpose:** prevent communication mistakes from being confused with scientific evidence and keep external review human-led.

## 1. Human authority

External scientific contact is human-initiated.

AI tools may help with literature search, source checking, translation, drafting, proofreading, code, and mathematical stress testing. They must not independently decide whom to contact, initiate outreach, or send follow-up messages.

For every first contact and every follow-up:
- a human chooses the recipient;
- a human chooses the purpose of the contact;
- a human reviews the final text;
- a human explicitly authorizes sending.

No autonomous recipient discovery-to-send loop is permitted.

## 2. First-contact format

The first message should read like ordinary correspondence between people.

Prefer:
- real name and a plain description of who is writing;
- one concise mathematical question;
- one short human-readable note or manuscript link;
- a clear statement of what kind of feedback would be useful.

Avoid in first contact unless directly necessary:
- internal JANUS terminology;
- gate names, seals, replay names, lineage identifiers, or registry vocabulary;
- long lists of repository artifacts;
- machine-style verdict menus;
- claims about P versus NP that are not the actual subject of the requested review.

AI assistance should not be hidden when it is materially relevant, but the message must make clear that the human sender personally initiated and authorized the contact.

## 3. Review material

Use a layered structure:

1. **Human review note:** short, conventional mathematical exposition.
2. **Standalone manuscript:** full proof candidate.
3. **Source ledger / replay artifacts:** provenance and audit material, supplied only when useful.

Internal proof-carrying infrastructure is evidence for reproducibility; it is not a substitute for readable mathematical exposition.

## 4. Contact boundaries

A recipient who asks not to receive further messages must not be contacted again unless that person later initiates contact or explicitly reverses the request.

Do not publish a public list of people who opted out. Keep any do-not-contact state in the appropriate private communication/Observer state.

Do not send repeated follow-ups merely because no answer was received. Silence is not evidence and does not create a scientific gate transition.

## 5. Scientific firewall

The following are communication events, not scientific validation:
- a reply;
- interest;
- a referral;
- willingness to talk;
- a meeting;
- repository access;
- a request for clarification.

Scientific status may change only when new substantive mathematical evidence is supplied: a proof check, exact gap, counterexample, prior-art theorem, corrected derivation, independent reproduction, or comparable content.

A negative reaction to the communication method is likewise not a mathematical refutation.

## 6. External-review status vocabulary

For external review, prefer ordinary descriptions:
- review requested;
- review not performed;
- substantive mathematical feedback received;
- gap identified;
- correction requested;
- independent check supports the argument.

Do not infer a theorem seal from politeness, interest, or lack of objection.

## 7. E8 application

For the current E8 candidate:
- the old machine-oriented review packet remains provenance only;
- the preferred external entry point is the short human-readable review note;
- no further outreach is automatic;
- any future recipient must be chosen and contacted personally by the human author;
- the candidate theorem remains unsealed until substantive independent mathematical review is obtained.
