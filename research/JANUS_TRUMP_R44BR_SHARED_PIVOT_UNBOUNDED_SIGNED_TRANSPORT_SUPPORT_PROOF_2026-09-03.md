# R44BR — Shared-pivot family forces unbounded signed-transport support

## Base data inherited from R44AS/R44BQ

Let `F` be the six-variable R44AS parent. It is maxdef-critical with `delta*(F)=2`; every proper clause-subset has deficiency at most `1`. Branching on pivot `x2` yields siblings `A` and `B` with `delta*(A)=delta*(B)=1`.

R44BQ exact replay establishes two local facts used here:

1. the identity map is not a valid signed clause-subsumption transport in either sibling direction;
2. a valid `B -> A` signed transport exists on the five nonpivot variables.

## Coupled family

For integer `k>=1`, take `k` renamed copies of `F`. Keep all five nonpivot variables disjoint between copies, but identify all copy-pivots with one shared variable `x`. Call the result `G_k`.

After setting `x`, all blocks separate:

`G_k[x=0] = A_1 ⊎ ... ⊎ A_k`,

`G_k[x=1] = B_1 ⊎ ... ⊎ B_k`.

## Theorem 1 — exact parent rank and criticality

For any clause-subformula `S⊆G_k`, let `S_i` be its clauses from block `i`. In the corresponding local copy let

- `d_i = |S_i|-|Var_local(S_i)|`, where the shared pivot is counted locally when it occurs;
- `p_i=1` iff the pivot occurs in `S_i`, otherwise `0`.

Since all nonpivot variables are block-disjoint while all used pivots are identified globally,

`delta(S) = sum_i d_i + sum_i p_i - 1_{sum_i p_i>0}`.

For a full local block, `(d_i,p_i)=(2,1)`. For every proper local clause-subset, `d_i<=1` by R44AS criticality.

Hence the full formula has

`delta(G_k)=2k+k-1=3k-1`.

Any proper global clause-subset has at least one proper local block. If the shared pivot is present, replacing one full local contribution `d_i+p_i=3` by a proper contribution at most `2` reduces the total by at least one before the common final `-1`. If the pivot is absent globally, every block is proper and the deficiency is even smaller. Therefore every proper clause-subset has deficiency at most `3k-2`.

Thus

`delta*(G_k)=3k-1`,

and `G_k` is maxdef-critical.

## Theorem 2 — exact sibling ranks

The two siblings are variable-disjoint unions of `k` copies of `A` or `B`. Maximum deficiency is additive under variable-disjoint union because every clause-subset partitions uniquely among the components and ordinary deficiency adds componentwise. Since each local child has maxdef `1`,

`delta*(G_k[x=0]) = delta*(G_k[x=1]) = k`.

So the shared-pivot split is a genuine critical branchwise descent:

`3k-1 -> k` on both children.

## Theorem 3 — every signed transport has support at least k

Consider a valid signed-permutation clause-subsumption transport in either direction between the two siblings.

Assume some target block `j` is pointwise fixed by the signed permutation. Then every target clause in that block is mapped to a clause over exactly the same block variables. Source blocks are variable-disjoint, so any source clause that is a subset of such an image must belong to source block `j`. Therefore restriction to block `j` would give the identity local transport between the base sibling pair in the same direction.

R44BQ excludes the identity local transport in both directions. Contradiction.

Hence every one of the `k` target blocks contains at least one moved variable. Target block variable sets are disjoint, therefore

`|supp(pi)| >= k`.

In the `B_k -> A_k` direction a transport exists: apply the R44BP five-variable signed permutation independently inside every block and identity nowhere else. Hence support is at most `5k` in that direction.

Consequently the minimum required signed-permutation support is unbounded as `k -> infinity`.

## Corollary — no universal constant support bound

For every fixed constant `K`, choose `k>K`. Then the maxdef-critical sibling pair generated by `G_k` admits no signed-permutation safe-delete certificate of support at most `K` in either direction.

Therefore no universal constant `K` can make the fixed-support discovery scheme of R44BQ complete over all maxdef-critical sibling pairs.

## Claim ceiling

This is a barrier for the **signed-permutation transport class with constant support bound**. It does not prove hardness of discovering arbitrary unbounded-support signed transports, and it does not refute substitution, affine, circuit, or other model-transport relations.
