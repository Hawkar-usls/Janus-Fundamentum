## 1. Purpose

The current exact representation is

    sparse affine equations over F_2
    +
    Boolean rank-one identities
        Z_ij = Z_0i Z_0j.

The anchored identities form a canonical O(N^2) obstruction basis, but the
missing step is an exact polynomial solver that remains tractable as those
nonlinear cuts are absorbed.

Before inventing such a solver, this audit checks the closest public donor
families.

## 2. Recursive AIP / vector-minion hierarchy

Barto, Hadek and Zhuk, "Toward a Uniform Algorithm and Uniform Reduction for
Constraint Problems", arXiv:2604.06335v1 (2026), introduce the vector
hierarchy and identify it with higher-level AIP over Z_p.

The paper separately states that AIP can be enhanced by a natural recursion,
that this direction appears genuinely different from singleton and
higher-level methods, and that formal results will appear in a separate paper.

Public searches through 2026-09-23 did not locate that promised separate
recursive-AIP paper.

Consequence:

    published vector hierarchy
    !=
    missing recursive donor.

JANUS does not attribute RAIL to the unpublished follow-up.

## 3. 2-XNF / products of linear Boolean forms

Andraschko, Danner and Kreuzer,
"SAT Solving Using XOR-OR-AND Normal Forms",
Mathematics in Computer Science 18, article 20 (2024), prove that every CNF
formula is equisatisfiable with a 2-XNF formula and explain that quadratic
Boolean systems can be transformed into systems of products of at most two
linear Boolean polynomials.

This is extremely close in representation spirit to the current minor gate:
nonlinearity is retained as compact products of linear forms.

However their solver is explicitly DPLL-like: it propagates algebraic/graph
information and performs guesses until the remaining ideal is generated by
linear polynomials.

Therefore:

    compact quadratic/product representation
    =
    SOURCE DONOR FOR REPRESENTATION

but

    deterministic polynomial exact solver
    =
    NOT SUPPLIED.

This is an important anti-loop: re-expressing the anchored minors as 2-XNF or
products of linear forms is not by itself a D1 candidate.

Public source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11786-024-00594-x

## 4. General quadratic Boolean solving

Bardet, Faugere, Salvy and Spaenlehauer,
"On the Complexity of Solving Quadratic Boolean Systems",
arXiv:1112.6263, study general MQ over F_2 and obtain exponential-time
algorithms using exhaustive search plus sparse linear algebra.

This literature does not supply the required polynomial solver for the JANUS
family.

It reinforces the correct interpretation:

    linear-size degree-2 representation
    !=
    polynomial-time decision algorithm.

## 5. Rank-one linear-matrix literature

Zhao and Fukushima,
"Rank-one Solutions for Homogeneous Linear Matrix Equations over the Positive
Semidefinite Cone", arXiv:1211.2131 / Applied Mathematics and Computation 219
(2013), develop sufficient conditions for real PSD homogeneous linear matrix
equations.

That problem is not the frozen JANUS problem:

- the field is different;
- PSD geometry is essential there;
- their conditions are sufficient/structural, not a complete polynomial
  algorithm for arbitrary finite-field Boolean rank-one completion.

Therefore it is background only, not an admissible solver donor.

## 6. Generic CEGAR / abstraction refinement

CEGAR is established prior art. It can iteratively remove spurious abstract
solutions, but applications to hard logical problems commonly invoke SAT/QBF
solvers inside the refinement loop.

Thus the generic existence of a refinement architecture does not establish a
polynomial lifecycle.

RAIL's extra polynomial-obstruction-basis contract is useful, but even after
the O(N^2) rank-one basis has been proved, the refined-state solver must still
be constructed independently.

## 7. Fixed-level moment escalation anti-loop

A tempting response to the nonlinear anchored minors is to lift again to
higher moments and repeat.

Known hierarchy lower bounds prevent treating this as a free universal
escape.

Kothari, Mori, O'Donnell and Witmer,
"Sum of squares lower bounds for refuting any CSP", arXiv:1701.04521, prove
degree lower bounds for broad random CSP families.

Hopkins and Lin,
"Explicit Lower Bounds Against Omega(n)-Rounds of Sum-of-Squares",
arXiv:2204.11469, construct explicit 3-XOR families hard for Omega(n) SoS
levels.

Scope:

These papers do not rule out every new representation-changing algorithm.
They do rule out promoting a fixed/low-level standard SoS-style moment
escalation merely because another polynomial-size moment layer can be written.

## 8. Source-first verdict

No public source located in this audit supplies the exact missing edge

    SPARSE_F2_AFFINE_RANK1_COMPLETION
    ->
    deterministic polynomial exact solver

for the full JANUS-generated family.

The closest useful donors divide into:

    recursive AIP:
        PROMISING BUT FORMAL FOLLOW-UP NOT PUBLICLY LOCATED

    2-XNF / s-Res:
        COMPACT REPRESENTATION + DPLL/GUESSING
        NOT D1

    MQ algebra:
        GENERAL EXPONENTIAL METHODS
        NOT D1

    PSD rank-one theory:
        WRONG FIELD / DIFFERENT GEOMETRY
        NOT DIRECTLY APPLICABLE

    CEGAR:
        META-ARCHITECTURE
        NO POLYNOMIAL BOUND BY ITSELF

    fixed-level moment/SOS:
        HARD-INSTANCE LOWER-BOUND CONTROLS
        NOT A FREE ESCAPE

Therefore the active mathematical target remains:

    R5_E8_6I_RAIL_MINOR_ABSORPTION_GATE_V1

with the additional anti-loop requirement:

    do not solve the gate merely by adding a fixed number
    of higher moment levels or by hiding DPLL guesses in a
    product-of-linear-forms representation.
