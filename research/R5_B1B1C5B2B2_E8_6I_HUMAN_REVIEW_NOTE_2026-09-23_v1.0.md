# Effective construction of short pp-definitions in a residually-small cube-term regime

**Status:** candidate result; external mathematical review still required.

## 1. Question

Bulín–Kompatscher, arXiv:2305.01984v3, prove in Theorem 25 that under their residual-finiteness hypothesis invariant relations in the few-subpowers / edge-term setting admit pp-definitions of polynomial length. Their Question 30 (Section 6.3) asks whether such a short definition can be constructed in polynomial time when the relation is given by generators.

The candidate below addresses a restricted version of that question.

## 2. Candidate statement

Let \(\mathcal K\) be a fixed finite family of finite similar algebras with a common \(k\)-cube term (equivalently a common \(k\)-edge term), and assume that \(\mathsf V(\mathcal K)\) is residually small.

For
\[
R=\operatorname{Sg}(a^1,\ldots,a^m)\le A_1\times\cdots\times A_n,
\qquad A_i\in\operatorname{HS}(\mathcal K),
\]
given by generators, the candidate algorithm constructs, in time polynomial in the encoded generator input, a pp-definition \(\varphi_R\) over a fixed finite relational basis depending only on \(\mathcal K\), such that
\[
\varphi_R(\bar x)\iff \bar x\in R
\]
and
\[
|\varphi_R|=O\!\left(n^{\max(2,k-1)}\right).
\]

This is not claimed as a proved theorem yet.

## 3. Why the statement is not immediate from the known results

Theorem 25 of Bulín–Kompatscher supplies the polynomial-size existence theorem. What is missing is an effective construction from generators without expanding the whole relation.

The proposed route uses the polynomial algorithms from Bulatov–Mayr–Szendrei (BMS) for compact representations and subpower membership, while using the Bulín–Kompatscher signature only as a counting/termination argument. The BMS compact representation and the Bulín–Kompatscher compact representation are not identified.

Residual smallness in the present fixed finite setting supplies the finite residual universe needed for the Bulín–Kompatscher recursion.

## 4. Construction outline

### 4.1 Low-arity shell

For every coordinate set \(I\) of size below the fixed threshold \(\max(k,3)\), compute the exact projection \(R|_I\). Because \(\mathcal K\) and the arity bound are fixed, these projection tables have constant-size ambient domains and can be recovered using polynomially many subpower-membership tests.

Intersect the corresponding cylinders. Using BMS compact-representation machinery, this intersection remains computable in polynomial time. The number of shell constraints is
\[
O\!\left(n^{\max(2,k-1)}\right).
\]

### 4.2 A constructive separator problem

The main algorithmic subproblem is the following.

Given an invariant relation \(S\) and \(y\notin S\), find
\[
t\notin S
\quad\text{with}\quad
y\notin\operatorname{Sg}(S,t),
\]
or certify that no such \(t\) exists.

Call such a tuple a safe extension of \(S\) relative to \(y\).

Repeated safe extensions produce a maximal invariant superrelation still excluding \(y\). If no safe extension remains, adjoining \(y\) gives its unique cover; this is the constructive analogue needed for the critical-relation decomposition used in the short-definition proof.

The polynomial bound on the number of strict extensions is obtained from the Bulín–Kompatscher signature/projection potential. The algorithm does not need to compute that full signature.

### 4.3 Localization to BMS coherence blocks

Assume the low-arity projections already pass for \(y\). Apply the BMS SI expansion and its large coherence blocks.

For an old large block \(E\), let
\[
T_E=S[\theta^E]
\]
be the corresponding product-congruence saturation / canonical inverse-image factor.

If \(y\) fails on \(E\), then \(y\notin T_E\). There are two cases.

1. If \(S<T_E\), any tuple in \(T_E\setminus S\) is immediately a global safe extension, because adjoining it remains inside \(T_E\), which still excludes \(y\).

2. If \(S=T_E\), strictness is visible on the block itself. The problem reduces to finding a strict liftable safe extension in the associated BMS coherent instance.

The completeness argument uses the fact that after adjoining a tuple, the new coherence partition refines the old one: enlarging a pair relation can destroy a graph relation but cannot turn a previously non-graph subdirect pair relation into a graph. Therefore a post-extension block witnessing failure of \(y\) lies inside an old block that the algorithm enumerates.

This localization step is one of the two places where I most want an independent check.

### 4.4 The coherent case

Inside one old coherent skeleton, BMS Theorem 2.2 gives abelian-group structure on the relevant congruence classes, and BMS Claims 6.6–6.7 characterize target membership through the subgroup generated by unary-polynomial contributions.

After quotienting out the contributions already present in \(S\), the effect of a candidate new generator is represented by a fixed finite family of induced homomorphisms
\[
L:N_q\to M_y.
\]

For a displacement \(v\) of the new generator, the unsafe condition has the form
\[
L(v)=\bar y
\]
for at least one map from a constant-size family. Hence the unsafe set is a finite union of affine cosets of kernels of homomorphisms between finite abelian groups.

Because the number of relevant map types depends only on the fixed family \(\mathcal K\), exact finite-group linear algebra and constant-size inclusion-exclusion give a polynomial procedure for deciding whether a safe displacement exists and recovering one.

The exact passage from BMS Claims 6.6–6.7 to this cross-fibre affine description is the second place where I most want an independent check.

### 4.5 Quotient-changing extensions

A safe extension may also change the quotient skeleton. The proposed normalization shows that it is enough to enumerate one original coordinate bundle on which the quotient graph is broken, while solving the remaining displacement problem in the old coherent module.

Because each original coordinate algebra is fixed finite, only constantly many values must be tried for a chosen bundle, and there are only \(O(n)\) bundles.

### 4.6 Return to the Bulín–Kompatscher recursion

Once critical separators can be constructed rather than merely shown to exist, the arity-reduction step in the Bulín–Kompatscher proof becomes effective.

Images of generator sets under the relevant quotient maps generate the quotient relation, and the BMS SMP/compact-representation equivalence allows recompaction after every recursive step.

The intended result is therefore a polynomial-time compiler
\[
\text{generators}\longrightarrow\text{short pp-definition}
\]
with the same asymptotic pp-size scale as the existence theorem.

## 5. Points that need independent verification

The full candidate stands or falls on ordinary mathematics, not on internal testing. The points I would most value having checked are:

1. **Old-block localization.** Does the refinement argument really ensure that every global safe extension is detected by one of the old BMS blocks, with \(T_E=S[\theta^E]\) serving exactly as the strictness detector described above?

2. **Cross-fibre affine avoidance.** Do BMS Theorem 2.2 and Claims 6.6–6.7 justify the claimed fixed family of induced homomorphisms and the finite-union-of-affine-cosets description of all unsafe one-generator extensions?

3. **Quotient-changing normalization.** Is the reduction to one changed original bundle complete, including liftability back to the original coordinates?

4. **Complexity accounting.** Is every construction polynomial in the original encoded generator input, with no hidden enumeration of the full relation and no representation-conversion assumption not supplied by BMS?

A pointer to the first incorrect step, if there is one, would be more useful than a broad assessment.

## 6. Scope

Even if correct, this would be a constructive result inside a tractable few-subpowers regime. It would not by itself give a polynomial algorithm for arbitrary SAT and would not resolve P versus NP.
