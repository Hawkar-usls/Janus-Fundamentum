## 2. Ternary kernel gains exactly one constant-frequency mode

Now work over `F3`.
Because `q>3` is prime, `gcd(q,3)=1`, so the translation action of `F_q` is semisimple after extending scalars to an algebraic closure of `F3` containing a primitive `q`-th root of unity.
The usual additive Fourier decomposition is therefore valid for rank/nullity counting.

At one translation frequency `chi_k`, write

\[
y_s(t)=a_s\chi_k(t).
\]

The source row equation is

\[
(1+\chi_k(s))a_s+\chi_k(2s)a_{-2s}=0.
\]

Since `chi_k(2s)` is nonzero, one amplitude determines all amplitudes around the single orbit `O`.  Thus every frequency contributes kernel dimension at most one.

### Zero frequency

For `k=0`, `chi_0=1`, so

\[
2a_s+a_{-2s}=0.
\]

In characteristic three, `-2=1`, hence

\[
a_{-2s}=a_s.
\]

Because `O` is one orbit, the zero-frequency kernel is exactly one-dimensional, generated by the all-ones arc vector.

### Nonzero frequencies

For every `k != 0`, the gradient of the potential `p(t)=chi_k(t)` has amplitudes

\[
a_s=\chi_k(s)-1.
\]

They are nonzero for every nonzero `s`, and

\[
(\chi_k(s)-1)(1+\chi_k(s))
+(\chi_k(-2s)-1)\chi_k(2s)=0
\]

identically.  Hence every nonzero frequency contributes exactly one dimension.

There are `q-1` nonzero frequencies. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\nu_{F3}(A_q)=q.
}
\]

Equivalently, the ternary kernel is the direct sum of the `(q-1)`-dimensional gradient space and the constant all-ones mode.

\[
