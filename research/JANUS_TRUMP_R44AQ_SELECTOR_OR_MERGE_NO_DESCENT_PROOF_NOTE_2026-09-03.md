# JANUS TRUMP R44AQ — Selector OR merge and selector-free CNF projection

## 1. Exact selector merge

For CNFs `A` and `B`, introduce fresh selector `s` and define

`M_s(A,B) = {s OR C : C in A} union {not s OR D : D in B}`.

Then `SAT(M_s(A,B)) iff SAT(A) OR SAT(B)`. For `A=F[v=0]` and `B=F[v=1]`, this is Shannon reconstruction of `F` with `v` renamed to `s`; it does not remove the binary degree of freedom.

## 2. Exact selector-free projection back to CNF

Let the current CNF contain clauses `(x OR C_i)`, `(not x OR D_j)`, and clauses `R` not containing `x`. Existentially eliminating `x` while remaining in CNF yields

`R AND AND_{i,j}(C_i OR D_j)`,

after deleting tautological resolvents and applying ordinary simplifications. This is precisely Davis-Putnam variable elimination / resolution on pivot `x`.

One elimination is polynomial in the current formula size: at most `|P_x|*|N_x|` pivot resolvents are generated. This does **not** imply a polynomial trajectory relative to the original input.

## 3. Unconditional route barrier

The classical Davis-Putnam lower-bound literature contains infinite contradictory CNF families for which any variable elimination order generates exponentially many distinct clauses. The relevant published record is the 1977 TCS paper `On the complexity of regular resolution and the Davis-Putnam procedure`, DOI `10.1016/0304-3975(77)90054-8`, whose abstract states a `2^{c n}` lower bound on the number of distinct clauses generated by Davis-Putnam for the constructed family under any elimination order.

Therefore exact selector-free projection **inside ordinary CNF** does not supply the Legend state/work bound.
