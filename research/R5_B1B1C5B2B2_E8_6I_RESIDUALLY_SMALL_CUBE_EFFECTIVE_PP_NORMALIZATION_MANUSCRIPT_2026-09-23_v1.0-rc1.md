## 1. Candidate theorem

Let \\(\\mathcal K\\) be a fixed finite family of finite similar algebras with a common \\(k\\)-cube term (equivalently, a common \\(k\\)-edge term), and assume \\(\\mathsf V(\\mathcal K)\\) is residually small.

For
\\[
R=\\operatorname{Sg}(a^1,\\ldots,a^m)\\le A_1\\times\\cdots\\times A_n,
\\qquad A_i\\in\\operatorname{HS}(\\mathcal K),
\\]
given by generators, the candidate algorithm constructs in deterministic time polynomial in the encoded generator-input length \\(L\\) a pp-definition \\(\\varphi_R\\) over one fixed finite relational basis \\(\\Gamma_{\\mathcal K}\\), with
\\[
\\varphi_R(\\bar x)\\iff \\bar x\\in R
\\]
and
\\[
|\\varphi_R|=O\\!\\left(n^{\\max(2,k-1)}\\right).
\\]

The proof uses BMS standardized compact representations operationally and Bulín–Kompatscher (BK) full signatures only as mathematical counting potentials. These two representation notions are not identified.

This manuscript does **not** claim THEOREM_SEAL.

## 2. Exact source backbone

All theorem/lemma numbers below refer to the exact public versions named here. Older internal Janus numbering is non-authoritative.

### 2.1 Berman–Idziak–Marković–McKenzie–Valeriote–Willard

Source: *Varieties with few subalgebras of powers*, Trans. AMS 362 (2010).

- **Theorem 2.12:** for every fixed \\(k\\ge2\\), a variety has a \\(k\\)-cube term iff it has a \\(k\\)-edge term. Thus the same parameter \\(k\\) may be used at the BMS cube-term and BK edge-term interfaces.
- **Theorem 4.2:** a variety with a \\(k\\)-edge term is congruence modular.

### 2.2 Bulatov–Mayr–Szendrei (BMS)

Source: *The Subpower Membership Problem for Finite Algebras with Cube Terms*, LMCS 15(1:11), 2019.

- **Theorem 2.1(2):** a variety with a cube term is congruence modular.
- **Theorem 2.2:** abelian congruence classes carry induced abelian-group structures and term operations decompose linearly between their blocks.
- **Theorem 2.3:** for a finite algebra generating a congruence-modular variety, residual smallness is equivalent to a finite natural residual bound.
- **Theorem 3.6:** compact/standardized representation data reconstruct the represented subalgebra.
- **Theorem 3.12:** a product-congruence saturation \\(B[\\theta]\\) has an explicit generating set from local/fork witness data.
- **Theorem 4.10:** \\(\\operatorname{SMP}(\\mathcal K)\\) and \\(\\operatorname{SMP}(\\operatorname{HS}\\mathcal K)\\) are polynomial-time equivalent.
- **Theorem 4.16:** \\(\\operatorname{SMP}(\\mathcal K)\\) and \\(\\operatorname{CompactRep}(\\mathcal K)\\) are polynomial-time reducible to one another.
- **Theorem 5.3 / Remark 5.4:** after the required small projections, membership is characterized by projections to the large coherence blocks (equivalently, by the canonical factor intersection).
- **Definition 6.2:** definition of a \\(d\\)-coherent SMP input.
- **Theorem 6.4 + Algorithm 7:** SMP reduces polynomially to \\(d\\)-coherent instances over \\(\\operatorname{HS}(\\mathcal K)\\); the proof shows that each computed large block is such an input and that only \\(O(n)\\) block calls are made.
- **Theorem 6.5:** SMP is in P in the residually-small cube-term regime.
- **Claims 6.6–6.7:** Algorithm 8 constructs the unary-polynomial contributions, and target membership is equivalent to membership in the subgroup generated by those contributions.
- The complexity analysis after Claim 6.7 shows that the Algorithm-8 type/function universe is bounded by constants depending only on fixed \\(\\mathcal K\\).
- BMS also records that Dalmau's algorithm constructs a compact representation of an intersection from compact representations in polynomial time; together with Theorem 4.16 this supplies the intersection primitive used below.

### 2.3 Bulín–Kompatscher (BK), arXiv:2305.01984v3, 27 Jan 2026

Source: *Polynomial definability in constraint languages with few subpowers*.

- **Lemma 3.2:** fixed finite powers preserve the polynomial short-definition scale.
- **Lemma 3.3:** single-sorted short pp-definitions are equivalent, up to the same asymptotic scale, to multi-sorted short pp-definitions over the fixed factor family.
- **Theorem 3.4:** a BK compact representation generates the represented invariant relation.
- **Corollary 3.5:** for comparable invariant relations, equality of full BK signature plus all \\(<k\\) projections implies equality of the relations.
- **Theorem 4.1:** a critical relation of arity at least \\(k\\) has the parallelogram property.
- **Lemma 4.2:** the low-projection shell plus at most \\(n|A|^2\\) nonredundant critical parallelogram conjuncts suffices; quantitatively it yields the \\(O(n^{k-1}+n f(n))\\) reduction.
- **Lemma 4.3:** coordinate linkedness quotients reduce a nonreduced critical parallelogram relation to a reduced one, and the original relation is the full inverse image under the quotient maps.
- **Lemma 4.4:** every sort of a reduced critical parallelogram relation is subdirectly irreducible.
- **Lemma 4.5:** the linkedness congruence \\(\\theta_I\\) is meet-irreducible.
- **Theorem 4.6:** under residual finiteness, short pp-definitions have length \\(O(n^{\\max(2,k-1)})\\); its proof gives the explicit \\(A_{1,2},Q,R'\\) arity-reduction step and shows \\(A_{1,2}\\) is subdirectly irreducible.
- **Question 6.3:** asks for a polynomial-time construction of a short pp-definition from generators; v3 states that the answer is positive over the Boolean domain but unknown even in the context of residual finiteness.

## 3. Bridge Lemma 0 — Residual-small scope imports the BK residual-finite universe

**Lemma 0.** Under the hypotheses of the candidate theorem, \\(\\mathsf V(\\mathcal K)\\) satisfies the residual-finiteness hypothesis used by BK Theorem 4.6.

**Proof.** Let
\\[
\\mathbf A:=\\prod_{\\mathbf B\\in\\mathcal K}\\mathbf B.
\\]
Because \\(\\mathcal K\\) is finite, \\(\\mathbf A\\) is finite and
\\[
\\mathsf V(\\mathbf A)=\\mathsf V(\\mathcal K).
\\]
The common \\(k\\)-cube term is preserved by finite products; by BMS Theorem 2.1, \\(\\mathsf V(\\mathbf A)\\) is congruence modular. By assumption it is residually small. BMS Theorem 2.3 (the Freese–McKenzie residual-bound theorem in this setting) therefore gives a natural number \\(c\\) such that every subdirectly irreducible algebra in \\(\\mathsf V(\\mathbf A)\\) has cardinality \\(<c\\).

The language is fixed and finite. Hence, up to isomorphism, only finitely many finite algebras of cardinality \\(<c\\) exist in this language. Thus the subdirectly irreducible members of \\(\\mathsf V(\\mathcal K)\\) form, up to isomorphism, a finite family of finite algebras. This is precisely the residual-finiteness hypothesis used by BK Theorem 4.6. ∎

Consequently there is a fixed finite SI/factor universe and a fixed finite multisorted basis \\(\\Gamma_{\\mathcal K}\\).

## 4. Bridge Lemma 1 — Polynomial compact construction of the low-projection shell

Put
\\[
s:=\\max(k,3).
\\]
For every \\(I\\subseteq[n]\\) with \\(|I|<s\\), let
\\[
P_I:=\\operatorname{proj}_I(R)
\\]
and
\\[
C_I:=\\operatorname{proj}_I^{-1}(P_I).
\\]
Define
\\[
C_0:=\\bigcap_{|I|<s}C_I.
\\]

**Lemma 1.** From a BMS standardized compact representation of \\(R\\), a standardized compact representation of \\(C_0\\) is computable in polynomial time.

**Proof.**

1. **Materialize each fixed-arity table \\(P_I\\).**  
   Project the compact generators of \\(R\\) to \\(I\\). They generate \\(P_I\\). Since \\(|I|<s\\) and \\(s,\\mathcal K\\) are fixed, the ambient product on \\(I\\) has constant cardinality. Enumerate it and use SMP on the projected generators to recover the exact table \\(P_I\\) in constant-many polynomial membership tests.

2. **Construct a compact representation of each cylinder \\(C_I\\).**  
   Membership in \\(C_I\\) depends only on the constant-size coordinate set \\(I\\). The local witnesses and derived-fork witnesses required by a BMS standardized representation can therefore be constructed explicitly: for every required fixed-size local projection/fork pattern, enumerate the constant-size restriction on \\(I\\), choose a compatible tuple of \\(P_I\\), and fill coordinates outside the constrained set arbitrarily. The number of required witnesses is polynomial in \\(n\\).

3. **Intersect.**  
   BMS note that from compact representations of two subalgebras, Dalmau's algorithm constructs a compact representation of their intersection in polynomial time; together with Theorem 4.16 this is polynomial in the present scope. There are
   \\[
   \\sum_{i<s}\\binom ni=O(n^{s-1})
   \\]
   cylinders. Repeated intersection therefore yields compact \\(C_0\\) in polynomial time. ∎

The shell itself contributes
\\[
O(n^{s-1})=O(n^{\\max(2,k-1)})
\\]
fixed-arity pp-atoms.

For \\(k=2\\), the extra pair projections required by BMS localization raise the shell only to \\(O(n^2)\\), which is still the BK degree \\(\\max(2,k-1)=2\\).

## 5. Bridge Lemma 2 — Operational representation vs BK signature potential

BMS standardized representations and BK compact representations are **not** treated as identical.

- **Operational state:** BMS standardized compact representation, because BMS supplies the polynomial algorithms needed by the compiler.
- **Counting potential:** the abstract BK feature set
  \\[
  \\Phi(S):=\\operatorname{Sig}_{BK}(S)\\;\\cup\\;
  \\{(I,a):|I|<k,\\ a\\in\\operatorname{proj}_I(S)\\}.
  \\]

**Lemma 2.** Every strict invariant extension \\(S<T\\) changes \\(\\Phi\\).

**Proof.** If the BK signatures and all \\(<k\\) projections were equal, BK Corollary 19 would imply \\(S=T\\), contradiction. ∎

The number of possible features is
\\[
O(n|A|^2)+
\\sum_{i<k}\\binom ni O(|A|^i)
=
O(n^{\\max(2,k-1)})
\\]
for the fixed multisorted factor universe.

This is only a mathematical termination potential. The algorithm never needs to compute the full BK signature.

In the **outer** critical-decomposition chain, the \\(<k\\) projections have already been frozen by \\(C_0\\), so each nonredundant critical conjunct strictly decreases only the BK signature. BK Lemma 4.2 therefore gives the sharper \\(O(n)\\) bound.

## 6. Bridge Lemma 3 — Strict existential localization of SAFEEXT to old BMS blocks

Before SAFEEXT maximalization, freeze the coordinate sorts to
\[
A_i:=\operatorname{proj}_i(R).
\]
The unary shell atoms enforce these sorts in the eventual pp-definition. Hence every current invariant superrelation
\[
R\le S\le A_1\times\cdots\times A_n
\]
is subdirect on the frozen unary sorts.

Let \(S\) be such a current relation, let \(y\notin S\), and assume
\[
y|_I\in S|_I
\qquad
\text{for all }|I|<\max\{d,3\}.
\]
This holds in the outer construction because \(y\in C_0\) and \(R\le S\).

Apply the BMS SI expansion to \(S\). Let
\[
E_1,\ldots,E_m
\]
be the large old coherence blocks computed by Algorithm 7. By Theorem 5.3, because \(y\notin S\) and all small checks pass, at least one old block satisfies
\[
\hat y|_E\notin\hat S|_E.
\]
Algorithm 7 computes all large blocks in polynomial time, there are only \(O(n)\) of them, and the restriction to every such block is a valid d-coherent \(\operatorname{HS}(\mathcal K)\) instance.

For every old block \(E\), define
\[
\theta_i^E
:=
\bigcap\{\sigma:(i,\sigma)\in E\},
\]
with \(\theta_i^E=1_{A_i}\) when \(E\cap W_i=\varnothing\), and put
\[
\theta^E:=\theta_1^E\times\cdots\times\theta_n^E.
\]
The corresponding liftable canonical factor is
\[
T_E:=S[\theta^E].
\]
Equivalently,
\[
T_E
=
\{x\in A_1\times\cdots\times A_n:
\hat x|_E\in\hat S|_E\}.
\]
Thus for every old block with \(\hat y|_E\notin\hat S|_E\),
\[
S\le T_E
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y\notin T_E.
\]

BMS Theorem 3.12 gives a polynomial-size generating scheme for product-congruence saturations, Algorithm 3 implements the required construction in polynomial time for fixed \(\mathcal K\), and Theorem 4.16 recompacts the resulting generators polynomially. Therefore compact \(T_E\) is polynomially constructible.

### 6.1 Strictness detector

For every old y-failing block \(E\), perform the following case split.

**Case A: \(S<T_E\).**  
Extract
\[
t\in T_E\setminus S
\]
by polynomial compact comparison. Since \(T_E\) is a subalgebra,
\[
\operatorname{Sg}(S,t)\le T_E.
\]
Because \(y\notin T_E\),
\[
y\notin\operatorname{Sg}(S,t).
\]
Thus \(t\) is an immediate global SAFEEXT witness.

**Case B: \(S=T_E\).**  
Run the liftable strict SAFEEXT solver on the d-coherent restriction \(S|_E,y|_E\). A local witness must satisfy both
\[
u_E\notin S|_E
\]
and
\[
y|_E\notin\operatorname{Sg}(S|_E,u_E).
\]
If such a liftable \(u_E\) is found, choose an original-coordinate completion \(t\) with \(\hat t|_E=u_E\). Then \(t\notin S=T_E\). Moreover, if
\[
y\in\operatorname{Sg}(S,t),
\]
projection to \(E\) would imply
\[
y|_E\in\operatorname{Sg}(S|_E,u_E),
\]
contradiction. Hence the completed \(t\) is a global SAFEEXT witness.

### 6.2 Completeness of the block enumeration

Assume a global safe witness exists:
\[
t\notin S,
\qquad
C:=\operatorname{Sg}(S,t),
\qquad
y\notin C.
\]
All required small projection checks still pass for \(C\), since
\[
R\le S\le C
\]
and \(y\in C_0\).

Apply Theorem 5.3 to \(C\). There exists a large post-extension coherence block \(U\) such that
\[
\hat y|_U\notin\hat C|_U.
\]

The post-extension coherence relation refines the old one:
\[
\sim_C\subseteq\sim_S.
\]
Indeed, the unary SI factors are unchanged because the unary sorts are frozen. Pair quotient images only enlarge from \(S\) to \(C\). An old graph may remain a graph or cease to be one; an old non-graph cannot become a graph by enlargement, because an enlarged graph would contain the old subdirect pair relation as a subdirect subset and hence force that old pair relation to equal the graph already.

Therefore
\[
U\subseteq E
\]
for some old large block \(E\) of \(S\). If
\[
\hat y|_E\in\hat C|_E,
\]
projection from \(E\) to \(U\) would give \(\hat y|_U\in\hat C|_U\), contradiction. Hence
\[
\hat y|_E\notin\hat C|_E.
\]
Since
\[
\hat C|_E
=
\operatorname{Sg}(\hat S|_E,\hat t|_E),
\]
the projection \(t|_E\) is locally safe in the target-exclusion sense.

The algorithm now examines this same old block \(E\).

- If \(S<T_E\), Case A already yields an immediate global safe witness, independently of whether \(t|_E\) itself is strict.
- If \(S=T_E\), the equality makes strictness visible on \(E\): from \(t\notin S=T_E\) we get
  \[
  \hat t|_E\notin\hat S|_E.
  \]
  Together with \(\hat y|_E\notin\hat C|_E\), this says that \(t|_E\) is a **strict liftable local SAFEEXT witness** for the d-coherent block. Therefore the local solver of Lemmas B/C must return YES.

Thus, if a global safe extension exists, one of the polynomially many old y-failing blocks yields either an immediate safe witness from a proper liftable canonical factor or a strict liftable d-coherent SAFEEXT witness.

Conversely, either branch constructs a global safe witness by the arguments above.

Therefore:
\[
\operatorname{SAFEEXT}(S,y)
\]
is polynomially equivalent, in the frozen low-consistent scope, to the following polynomial block enumeration:

1. compute all old large BMS blocks;
2. retain the blocks \(E\) with \(\hat y|_E\notin\hat S|_E\);
3. construct compact \(T_E=S[\theta^E]\);
4. if \(S<T_E\), return an immediate global witness;
5. if \(S=T_E\), invoke the liftable strict d-coherent SAFEEXT solver on \(E\);
6. return NO only if every y-failing old block returns NO.

The saturation factor is used only as a **strictness detector**. No preselected-block equivalence
\[
\text{global SAFEEXT}\iff\text{local SAFEEXT on one fixed }E
\]
is asserted.

## 7. Lemma A — SAFEEXT and pointed maximal separators

For invariant \\(S\\) and \\(y\\notin S\\), define
\\[
\\operatorname{SAFEEXT}(S,y)
\\]
to return a tuple \\(t\\notin S\\) satisfying
\\[
y\\notin\\operatorname{Sg}(S\\cup\\{t\\}),
\\]
or certify that none exists.

Repeated safe extensions produce
\\[
S_0<S_1<\\cdots<S_m
\\]
while excluding \\(y\\). By Lemma 2, \\(m=O(n^{\\max(2,k-1)})\\). Recompact after every extension using SMP \\(\\leftrightarrow\\) CompactRep.

When no safe extension exists, every strict invariant superrelation of \\(S_m\\) contains \\(y\\). Therefore
\\[
S_m^*:=\\operatorname{Sg}(S_m\\cup\\{y\\})
\\]
is the unique cover of \\(S_m\\); hence \\(S_m\\) is completely meet-irreducible.

## 8. Lemma B — Old-skeleton cross-fibre affine avoidance

Let \\(B\\) be a d-coherent old-skeleton subalgebra, with product abelian congruence \\(\\rho\\) as in BMS Algorithm 8. Let \\(q\\) be an old quotient-skeleton class and let \\(q_y\\) be the class of the target \\(y\\).

Choose transversal representatives \\(o_q,o_y\\in O\\) and put
\\[
G_q=o_q/\\rho,\\quad K_q=B\\cap G_q,\\quad M_q=G_q/K_q,
\\]
\\[
G_y=o_y/\\rho,\\quad K_y=B\\cap G_y,\\quad M_y=G_y/K_y.
\\]

If the computation is taking place inside the SI expansion with bundle-compatibility restrictions, let \\(L_q\\subseteq G_q\\) denote the liftable part containing \\(K_q\\), and put
\\[
N_q:=L_q/K_q\\le M_q.
\\]
The liftable image is a subalgebra; unary polynomial maps with parameters in \\(B\\) preserve it. Hence every relevant induced linear map below restricts to \\(N_q\\).

Let
\\[
p_1,\\ldots,p_m
\\]
be the Algorithm-8 unary polynomial types whose output class is \\(q_y\\). Here \\(m=O_{\\mathcal K}(1)\\). By BMS Theorem 2.2,
\\[
p_i(x)=p_i(o_q)+L_i(x-o_q)
\\]
between the corresponding abelian blocks. Since the parameters and \\(o_q\\) lie in \\(B\\),
\\[
p_i(o_q)\\in K_y,
\\qquad
L_i(K_q)\\subseteq K_y.
\\]
Thus each \\(L_i\\) induces
\\[
\\bar L_i:N_q\\to M_y.
\\]

Let \\(e\\) be the lcm of the exponents of all induced abelian groups arising from the fixed family \\(\\mathcal K\\). This is a constant. Define
\\[
\\Lambda_{q\\to q_y}
:=
\\left\\{
\\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_i\\bar L_i:
c_i\\in\\mathbb Z/e\\mathbb Z
\\right\\}.
\\]
Therefore
\\[
|\\Lambda_{q\\to q_y}|\\le e^m=O_{\\mathcal K}(1).
\\]

For a candidate \\(t\\in L_q\\), write
\\[
v=(t-o_q)+K_q\\in N_q,
\\qquad
\\bar y=(y-o_y)+K_y\\in M_y.
\\]

**Lemma B.**
\\[
y\\in\\operatorname{Sg}(B,t)
\\iff
\\exists L\\in\\Lambda_{q\\to q_y}:L(v)=\\bar y.
\\]

**Proof.** BMS Claims 6.6–6.7 say that target membership is membership in the subgroup generated by the unary-polynomial contributions of the generators. After quotienting by the old target subgroup \\(K_y\\), all old contributions vanish. The only new contributions from \\(t\\) are \\(\\bar L_1(v),\\ldots,\\bar L_m(v)\\). Since the target group has exponent dividing \\(e\\),
\\[
\\langle\\bar L_1(v),\\ldots,\\bar L_m(v)\\rangle
=
\\left\\{
\\sum_i c_i\\bar L_i(v):c_i\\in\\mathbb Z/e\\mathbb Z
\\right\\}.
\\]
The right-hand side is exactly
\\[
\\{L(v):L\\in\\Lambda_{q\\to q_y}\\}.
\\]
This proves both directions. ∎

Hence the unsafe set inside the liftable source module is
\\[
\\operatorname{BAD}_{q,y}
=
\\{0\\}
\\cup
\\bigcup_{L\\in\\Lambda_{q\\to q_y}}
\\{v\\in N_q:L(v)=\\bar y\\}.
\\]
Each nonempty fibre is one affine coset of \\(\\ker L\\). Since \\(\\Lambda\\) is constant, exact counting of the union uses constant-size inclusion-exclusion and finite-abelian-group linear algebra. Coordinate self-reduction returns an explicit safe \\(v\\neq0\\) whenever one exists.

Thus old-skeleton SAFEEXT, including cross-fibre \\(q\\neq q_y\\), is polynomial-time decidable with a polynomial-time witness.

## 9. Lemma C — Quotient-changing SAFEEXT and one-original-bundle normalization

Work in the BMS SI expansion. Let the current d-coherent core \\(B\\) have old coherence block \\(E\\), and let
\\[
C=\\operatorname{Sg}(B,t)
\\]
for a globally safe quotient-changing tuple \\(t\\):
\\[
y\\notin C.
\\]

All required small-projection tests that held for \\(B\\) still hold for \\(C\\) because \\(B\\subseteq C\\). By BMS Theorem 5.3, there is a large post-extension coherence block
\\[
U\\subseteq E
\\]
such that
\\[
\\hat y|_U\\notin\\hat C|_U.
\\]

### Claim C1 — post-extension refinement

For SI coordinates \\(v,w\\in E\\), let \\(D_B(v,w)\\) and \\(D_C(v,w)\\) be their quotient-pair images. Then
\\[
D_B(v,w)\\le D_C(v,w).
\\]
If \\(D_B(v,w)\\) is the graph of the old isomorphism \\(\\phi_{vw}\\), it remains a graph after adjoining \\(t\\) iff the new quotient pair lies on that graph. A non-graph pair cannot become a graph by enlargement. Therefore
\\[
\\sim_C\\subseteq\\sim_B.
\\]
Inside \\(E\\), the new blocks are fibres of the constant-alphabet defect label induced by \\(t/\\rho\\).

Since \\(t\\) is quotient-changing and the old d-coherent core is one old quotient skeleton, \\(U\\) is proper.

### Claim C2 — original-bundle slice integrity

Let
\\[
W_j=\\{j\\}\\times\\operatorname{Irr}(B_j)
\\]
be an original-coordinate bundle. If
\\[
v,w\\in E\\cap W_j,
\\]
then their old pair quotient image is a graph. Because \\(B\\to B_j\\) is surjective, every element of \\(B_j\\)—in particular the candidate value \\(t_j\\)—satisfies this graph relation. Therefore all points of \\(E\\cap W_j\\) receive the same defect label.

Hence every post-extension block \\(U\\subseteq E\\) contains either the whole slice \\(E\\cap W_j\\) or none of it.

### Claim C3 — one-bundle normalization

Choose an original bundle \\(W_j\\) meeting \\(E\\setminus U\\), and put
\\[
V:=E\\setminus W_j.
\\]
By Claim C2,
\\[
U\\subseteq V.
\\]

The original restricted witness \\(t|_U\\) proves that an old-skeleton safe direction exists on \\(U\\). Apply Lemma B on the **liftable** source submodule to construct a liftable safe displacement \\(u\\) on \\(U\\).

Extend \\(u\\) to \\(V\\) by zero displacement relative to an old transversal representative on \\(V\\setminus U\\). Claim C2 makes this extension bundle-wise compatible: no original bundle is split between a new displacement and the old displacement. Call the lifted witness \\(u_V\\).

If
\\[
y|_V\\in\\operatorname{Sg}(B|_V,u_V),
\\]
then projection to \\(U\\) would imply
\\[
y|_U\\in\\operatorname{Sg}(B|_U,u),
\\]
contradicting safety. Hence \\(u_V\\) is safe on \\(V\\).

Finally choose a value in the omitted original coordinate \\(A_j\\) whose SI quotient labels violate at least one old quotient graph against \\(V\\). Such a value exists for the original quotient-changing \\(t_j\\). Since \\(A_j\\) is fixed finite, the algorithm need not know that original witness: it enumerates all constant-many values of \\(A_j\\) for each of the \\(O(n)\\) possible bundles \\(j\\), tests the quotient defect, and combines it with the polynomial module witness on \\(V\\).

Let the resulting full original tuple be \\(t'\\). The broken quotient graph gives
\\[
t'\\notin B.
\\]
If
\\[
y\\in\\operatorname{Sg}(B,t'),
\\]
projection to \\(V\\) would put \\(y|_V\\) in \\(\\operatorname{Sg}(B|_V,t'|_V)\\), contradiction. Thus \\(t'\\) is globally safe.

Therefore any quotient-changing safe extension is found by polynomially many one-bundle cases plus Lemma B. ∎

Bridge Lemma 3 enumerates all old y-failing large blocks. On each block, the saturation factor T_E is used only to decide whether strictness is already available globally or must be witnessed locally. Lemmas B and C solve the strict liftable d-coherent branch. Hence SAFEEXT is polynomial in the frozen residual-small cube-term scope.

## 10. Lemma D — Constructive dummy deletion

Let \\(S\\) be a maximal invariant superrelation of \\(R\\) excluding \\(y\\).

For coordinate \\(i\\), test the constant-many neighbours
\\[
y^{i\\leftarrow a},\\qquad a\\in A_i.
\\]

**Lemma D.**
\\[
i\\text{ is essential in }S
\\iff
\\exists a\\in A_i:
y^{i\\leftarrow a}\\in S.
\\]

If no such neighbour lies in \\(S\\), the invariant cylinder
\\[
\\operatorname{proj}_{-i}(S)\\times A_i
\\]
