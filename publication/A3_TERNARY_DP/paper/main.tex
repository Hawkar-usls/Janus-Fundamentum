\begin{abstract}
We study finite-field subspace arrangements containing $k$ distinct geometric subspace classes with arbitrary positive multiplicities.  In the admitted formal model, middle occurrences of a repeated class can be removed without changing the optimum path-width: only the first and last occurrence are required.  This yields an exact bottleneck dynamic program on endpoint states.  If $s$ classes occur once and $r$ classes occur at least twice, the state graph has exactly $2^s3^r$ states and $s2^{s-1}3^r+2r2^s3^{r-1}$ directed transitions.  After a $2^k$ subset-rank preprocessing table, the combinatorial dynamic-programming phase is $O(k3^k)$.  The project evidence includes independent brute-force controls and tamper tests.  Literature novelty is currently classified as a candidate rather than externally established.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Path-width of subspace arrangements appears in the structural study of trellis representations and matroid algorithms.  The purpose of this paper is deliberately restricted: we analyze arrangements with a small number $k$ of distinct geometric subspaces but arbitrary repetition.  The central observation is that repetition admits endpoint compression, after which exact optimization is naturally expressed as a ternary-state bottleneck dynamic program.

The result does not claim an improvement for unrestricted matroid path-width, does not overturn general hardness results, and does not claim external confirmation of historical novelty.  The accompanying machine-readable publication manifest fixes the precise theorem and claim ceiling used by this draft.

\section{Discussion}
The parameter $k$ is the number of distinct geometric subspace classes rather than the total number of occurrences.  Thus high multiplicity does not produce factorial occurrence-order search after endpoint compression.  The algorithm remains exponential in $k$, as expected for an FPT result parameterized by $k$.
